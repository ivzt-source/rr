# Call-in ad format (customer calls in with an outrageous request)

Produce an ad built as an **overheard phone call**: a host (radio/talk-back or podcast
style) or a **business owner at his desk** picks up, a customer makes an
**outrageous/absurd request**, and the exchange escalates until the **business's
capability or offer is the payoff**. The outrageousness is the hook; the business is the
competent straight-man that lands it.

This works because it fuses two proven engines: **sketch escalation comedy** and the
**parasocial credibility of a radio/podcast host**. Every rule below is grounded in the
research dossier at `../ad-formats-research/RESEARCH-DOSSIER.md`.

What the evidence says, in short:

- A felt one-sided relationship with a host (**parasocial interaction, PSI**) drives
  listening, confers **informational credibility** on the host, predicts **following the
  host's advice**, and measurably **influences attitudes** — this is why a host/owner
  answering calls can carry a product message believably.
- **Live-read, personality-voiced endorsements out-performed produced spots by ~40% on
  leads** in a documented radio case; majorities say they **trust** what a personality
  endorses and are likely to **talk about it**. The host-voice, in-world read is the
  engine — not a polished announcer.
- **Caution:** in a controlled corporate-podcast experiment, *stronger* parasocial
  bonding led to deeper processing that produced **negative** persuasive effects. Don't
  over-push the hard sell inside a high-rapport moment — let capability *demonstrate*,
  don't oversell.
- The call is a **sketch**: one game (the outrageous request), Set-up → Reveal →
  Escalation → Payoff, escalate variations of the same game, end on a reversal or a
  capability payoff.

---

## Step 0 — Pick the frame

Three interchangeable stagings; pick by what your AI pipeline renders most convincingly:

| Frame | Look | Native camouflage |
|---|---|---|
| **Talk-back radio** | Host at a mic, headphones, "ON AIR" light, caller as phone audio | Feels like a real radio clip |
| **Podcast** | Host(s) at a desk mic, caller patched in | Feels like a podcast cut-down |
| **Owner at his desk** | Business owner working, phone rings, he answers | Feels like a fly-on-the-wall moment |

All three exploit the **overheard-conversation effect**: audiences lean into a
conversation they feel they're eavesdropping on, and the humor + curiosity drive organic
reach as the clip blends natively into the feed.

---

## Step 1 — Define the one game: the outrageous request

The caller's request is the sketch's single game. Write it as one sentence:

> **"A customer calls [business] and demands [absurd request] — and won't take a
> reasonable answer."**

Good outrageous requests share three traits:
1. **Rooted in a real customer desire, pushed past the line.** ("Can you deliver…" →
   "…to a moving car on the highway?") The exaggeration must point at a *genuine* thing
   the business is good at.
2. **Escalatable.** Obvious "even more absurd" versions exist.
3. **The business, not the caller, ends up looking capable.** The absurdity attaches to
   the *caller*; the business is the straight-man who can somehow handle it. (See Step 6
   — this is the trust rule.)

---

## Step 2 — Cast the two-hander

- **The caller (unreasonable character):** the engine of the game. Distinct voice/accent,
  a confident-yet-ridiculous energy. Usually **audio-only** (phone), which is a gift for
  AI production — you only need a voice, not a rendered face.
- **The host/owner (straight character):** reasonable, calm, competent — the audience
  surrogate and the parasocial anchor. This is your **recurring, named** character; the
  face and voice the audience bonds with over time. Invest in making them likeable and
  consistent.

For AI production, lock the host's name, look, voice, desk/set, and a signature line
(e.g., how they answer the phone) into a character bible and reuse across every episode.

---

## Step 3 — SREP beat sheet for the call

Target 15–40s. Four beats:

1. **SET-UP (0–3s / HOOK).** The phone rings / "next caller" — and the outrageous request
   lands almost immediately. Front-load it: the absurd ask should be audible in the first
   1–2 seconds. (See Step 4.)
2. **REVEAL.** The host's first reaction registers the absurdity for the audience — a beat
   of deadpan, a repeat-back ("You want us to… *what*?"), or an unfazed "Sure, we can do
   that." The choice sets the comedic engine (see Step 5).
3. **ESCALATION (body).** The request gets *worse*, or the host's calm competence escalates
   against it. **3 beats**, each a notch more absurd, all variations of the same game — no
   new premises. Each turn: surprising in the moment, inevitable in hindsight.
4. **PAYOFF.** Two proven endings:
   - **Capability payoff:** the business *actually can* do the (slightly less) absurd
     thing — the offer is the answer that detonates the joke and doubles as the product
     proof. ("…and that's why we do same-day. What's the address?")
   - **Reversal:** the calm host finally out-absurds the caller, or gives in and matches
     their logic — turning the tables for the button laugh.

---

## Step 4 — Hook + call-audio treatment

**Hook (first 1–2s):** open *inside* the absurd request or on the host's reaction to it.
Highest-performing openers are **outrage/curiosity** lines:
- "You want us to cater a wedding… for tonight… for 400 people?"
- "So let me get this straight — you want a refund on a car you don't own?"
- Caption overlay of the request as a curiosity gap while the audio plays.

Never open with a jingle, logo, or "Welcome back to the show." The ring can be a single
beat; the request is the hook.

**Audio treatment (this sells the format):**
- **Band-limit / telephone-EQ the caller's voice** (thin, compressed, slightly distorted)
  so the "phone call" reads instantly and the two voices never blur.
- **Full, present, warm mic sound for the host** — this is the parasocial anchor; it must
  feel like a real person in the room / on the radio.
- Keep an "on-air"/room ambience bed low under the host. Optional: a subtle ring, a
  line-pickup click, a call-drop at the button.
- The **contrast** between phone-thin caller and warm-present host is what makes the
  overheard-call illusion work — treat it as a hard requirement, not polish.

---

## Step 5 — Choose the comedic engine

Two reliable dynamics; pick one per video:
- **Unflappable host:** the request escalates wildly, the host stays completely calm and
  competent. Comedy from the gap. Best for a **capability payoff** (the business can
  genuinely handle a lot).
- **Slow-burn host:** the host's composure cracks a little more each beat until the
  reversal. Best for a **reversal payoff**.

Keep the host *reasonable* either way — the audience must stay on their side. That
alignment is what preserves the parasocial credibility the CTA relies on.

---

## Step 6 — Land the outrage without denting trust

The outrageousness is the hook, but it must never make the *business* look bad. Rules:

1. **Point the absurdity at the caller, not the business.** The caller is the unreasonable
   character; the business is the capable straight-man. The business is never the butt.
2. **Anchor the exaggeration to a real strength.** The joke should be a stretched version
   of something the business genuinely does well, so the payoff proves a real capability.
3. **Don't over-sell in the high-rapport moment.** Strong parasocial bonding + a hard
   pitch can backfire (deeper processing → negative persuasion). Let the *capability*
   demonstrate the offer; keep the explicit ask light.
4. **Keep it clearly a bit.** Play it as obvious comedy, not a real complaint — you don't
   want viewers thinking the business actually mishandles customers.

---

## Step 7 — Place the CTA

- **Integrate it into the payoff**, in-character: the host's competent answer *is* the
  offer ("…which is exactly why we do 24/7 callouts — number's on screen").
- Personality-voiced, in-world reads out-perform produced-announcer tags. Say it as the
  host, not as a voiceover.
- A **direct-response mechanism** works well as the button: an on-screen number, a
  "text us", a handle. (A text-message CTA was the documented direct-response device in
  the radio case study.)
- One CTA. Late. Never before the payoff has landed.

---

## Step 8 — Franchise & cadence

- Make it a **recurring show**: same host, same "call-in" premise, a new outrageous caller
  each episode. The recurring host is the parasocial asset that compounds.
- Reuse the host's **signature phone-answer line** as the franchise's catchphrase.
- Hold a **consistent posting cadence** — inconsistency erodes the audience trust the
  format is built on.
- Keep the **character bible** (host name/look/voice/set/catchphrase; audio-EQ presets)
  so every episode is on-model — essential for AI-generated consistency.

---

## AI-character notes

- **The caller is audio-only** — cheap and safe to generate; spend variety here.
- **The host is your durable asset** — keep the face/voice rock-stable across episodes;
  model drift kills the parasocial bond. Over-invest in one great, consistent host.
- **Coherent, openly-virtual persona beats fake-realism.** Evidence on AI characters is
  mixed, not damning: a stable, transparent, likeable virtual host manages the
  authenticity/uncanny-valley risks better than trying to pass as a real human.
- **Disclosure is contested and jurisdiction-dependent** — decide once, then present any
  AI disclosure as part of a confident branded persona, not an apology. (Full nuance and
  citations in the dossier.)
- Because the format is voice-led, **casting a host voice that merely "fits the brand"
  isn't what builds the bond — capturing attention is.** Prioritize an attention-grabbing,
  likeable host performance over on-brand-but-bland.

---

## One-screen checklist

- [ ] Frame chosen (talk-back radio / podcast / owner-at-desk)
- [ ] Outrageous request = one game, rooted in a real business strength, escalatable
- [ ] Host is recurring, named, likeable, competent; caller has a distinct voice
- [ ] SREP beats; request audible in first 1–2s; 3 escalating beats; no new premises
- [ ] Caller voice telephone-EQ'd thin; host voice warm/present — hard contrast
- [ ] Comedic engine picked (unflappable host / slow-burn to reversal)
- [ ] Absurdity points at the caller; business stays the capable straight-man
- [ ] Payoff = capability proof or reversal; CTA integrated in-character, late, once
- [ ] Franchise: same host + signature answer line; consistent cadence
- [ ] Character bible + audio presets saved; AI persona coherent; disclosure decided

# Ad-format research dossier

Evidence base for the two ad-format skills:
- `../skit-ad-format/SKILL.md` — solo multi-character skit
- `../call-in-ad-format/SKILL.md` — customer call-in

**Method.** Compiled via a fan-out deep-research pass: 5 search angles → source fetch →
falsifiable-claim extraction → **adversarial 3-vote verification** (a claim needed ≥2 of
3 skeptical verifiers to *fail* to be killed). 78 claims were extracted from 23 sources;
the claims below are the ones that survived, plus an explicit list of what got **killed**
so we don't build on it. Where a source page was bot-blocked (403), quotes were recovered
from the search-engine index and cross-checked; those are flagged.

> indexed press, interviews, craft pedagogy, industry analyses, and peer-reviewed studies

## Part A — Solo multi-character skit (Format 1)

### A1. What made the exemplar's top posts work — timing vs craft
- Rees's **breakout virality was news-cycle-driven**: his "Meanwhile in Australia" series
  satirising COVID state-border restrictions is the content that "caught fire."
  [Illawarra Mercury]
- His career **originated in lockdown-era relatability** (parenting), not news satire —
  topicality came later. He pivoted from 11 years of children's TV (Giggle and Hoot) in
  2020 and it worked faster than he expected. [B&T; Illawarra Mercury]
- **But he sustained success with evergreen recurring-character franchises** not tied to
  the news: "The Brighton Ladies", "The Guy Who Decides", and POV videos with a "sassy"
  checkout-assistant reeling off observational lists. As of mid-2026, ~5M followers across
  platforms, organized around **named recurring franchises**. [named-franchise sources]
- **Takeaway for the skill:** topicality *accelerates* reach; recurring-character
  franchises *create the durable audience*. Both, but don't depend on news alone.

### A2. Relatability is the engagement nexus
- Rees attributes growth to capturing **relatable everyday moments**: "If you have these
  sort of relatable moments, [engagement] can just start growing." [B&T]
- B&T's session takeaway: **relatability is the nexus for engagement even for elevated
  comedy** like Rees's. [B&T]
- Co-panellist Maria Thattil: deliberately **less-polished branded Reels out-performed
  highly produced ones**. [B&T] — supports the "deliberate imperfection" heuristic.

### A3. Performance benchmarks
- 2021: Rees's Facebook videos logged **180M viewing minutes**, single clips routinely
  **>40M views**. Short-form skit audience converted to a **35-show sold-out arena tour**
  (booked as 5). [tour/benchmark sources]
- Trey Kennedy (other solo-multi-character exemplar): **>8.5M combined followers**; TikTok
  ~**400.8K views/post**; format described as **production-efficient and scalable — no
  heavy editing or collaborators** (directly relevant to an AI pipeline). Clean/
  family-friendly tone → "Safe" brand rating → mainstream brand deals. [Favikon]

### A4. Sketch craft (the beat structure the skill encodes)
- **One game:** "A sketch is a short, self-contained scene focused on one unusual thing
  that heightens to comedic absurdity." Build around **one idea** and exhaust it.
  [Mike Trapp / Chuffah]
- **Repetition with escalation:** "Something weird happens, and then it keeps happening."
  [Chuffah]
- **Heightening test:** each move should be "shocking in the moment, but in retrospect
  couldn't have happened any other way." [Chuffah]
- **SREP four-beat structure — Set-up, Reveal, Escalation, Payoff:** set-up establishes
  facts without telegraphing; reveal surprises with the central comic idea; escalation =
  variations on the same game with **no new ideas after the reveal**; **payoff** (not
  "punchline" — often a multi-line section). [Chris Head]
- **Reversal ending:** a stock sketch ending where the reasonable/straight character
  adopts the unreasonable character's behaviour. [Chris Head] — maps to the call-in host
  out-absurding the caller.
- **Self-contained:** sketches "require no knowledge of characters outside the script and
  cultural landscape" — relevant to whether non-recognizable AI characters can carry it.
  [Chuffah]

### A5. Production craft
- **Timing is made in the edit:** cut "just a few frames after the person has finished
  speaking," using the audio waveform to find the stop. [Gold Comedy]
- **Framing + editing > camera:** "a well-framed, well-edited video shot on a cellphone
  will be infinitely more watchable than a sloppily framed one." Light the face (2–3
  off-camera lights, or film beside a window; ring light = cheapest upgrade). [Gold Comedy]
- ⚠️ **KILLED claim:** "sketch videos follow a fixed wide→medium→close-up shot grammar and
  deviating lowers quality." Verifiers refuted this as an overreach of a soft heuristic —
  so the skill says *consistency and legibility* matter, not a mandatory grammar.

### A6. Skit ads as a marketing format
- Skit ads explicitly include the **solo-multi-character subtype** ("one person plays
  multiple characters"). Stated best practice: **clear visual OR vocal distinction between
  roles** (costumes, accents, mannerisms). [TryTheseAds]
- Skit-ad beat structure: **everyday relatable situation → product integrated organically
  → strong resolution (punchline/twist/CTA).** [TryTheseAds]
- Why they perform: **native-format camouflage** (blend into TikTok/Instagram feeds) +
  **shareability** drives organic reach beyond paid. [TryTheseAds]
- Hook pattern: **outrage/curiosity first line** — e.g. "I caught my roommate paying for 3
  Netflix accounts?!"; "I got fired for saving people too much money." [TryTheseAds]
- **Familiarity mechanics:** brand skits drive engagement via **recurring characters,
  catchphrases, repeatable setups** audiences "recognize and seek out." **Consistency of
  cadence is critical — inconsistency erodes audience trust.** [The Idea Farm]
- **The format transfers to branded content:** Rees's **"Meanwhile in Agency Land"** for
  the Google Premier Partner Awards 2021 kept the one-performer dual-role mechanic, swapped
  news satire for **in-group profession satire** (agency clichés), and ran **~4 min** —
  showing the format scales to longer B2B/branded pieces. [Branding in Asia]
- **Small businesses can do it too:** Duolingo's mascot "sitcom" strategy → 7M followers,
  16.5% TikTok engagement; **Mohawk Chevrolet**'s "The Dealership" (Office-style, real
  employees) → weekly series, 1M+ likes. [brand-skit sources]

## Part B — Customer call-in (Format 2)

### B1. Parasocial credibility of the host (why the format persuades)
Source: Rubin & Step (2000), *Journal of Broadcasting & Electronic Media* 44(4) —
peer-reviewed, hierarchical regression on public-affairs talk-radio audiences. Confirmed
claims:
- **PSI + entertainment motivation predicted intentional, frequent listening** to a
  favorite host.
- **PSI predicted following the host's advice to act** — i.e., parasocial bond →
  compliance with calls to action (direct read on CTA effectiveness).
- **PSI + task attraction + info motivation → regarding the host as an important
  information source** (host-answering-calls character can carry a product message
  credibly).
- **PSI predicted the host influencing attitudes** — measurable persuasive impact, not
  just entertainment.

### B2. The persuasion caution (don't over-sell)
Source: corporate-podcast lab experiment, *Journal of Radio & Audio Media* (2024), N=129,
2×2 between-subjects — peer-reviewed. Confirmed:
- **Attention** (not "voice-fit with the company") **increases PSI** with the host.
- **Higher PSI → deeper message processing → NEGATIVE persuasive effects.**
- **Skill implication:** cast for an attention-grabbing, likeable host, not a "on-brand"
  voice; and **let capability demonstrate the offer — keep the explicit pitch light**,
  because a hard sell inside a high-rapport moment can backfire.

### B3. Personality endorsement out-performs produced spots
Source: Cumulus Media / Blue Cross case study + 2014 USC/Clear Channel survey [Inside
Radio]. Confirmed:
- **Live-read personality endorsements = 40% more leads than produced spots**, 10% better
  than pre-recorded, ~250 leads/week.
- Campaign (Meers Advertising, targeting millennials) used **popular hosts + a
  text-message CTA** as the direct-response device.
- 2014 survey: **>50% trust brands a personality endorses; >60% likely to talk about it**
  (word-of-mouth amplification).
- **Skill implication:** the host-voiced, in-world read *is* the engine; CTA as a direct-
  response mechanism (on-screen number / "text us") placed in the payoff.

### B4. Sketch craft applies to the call
The call is a sketch: the **outrageous request is the one game**; use SREP, escalate
variations of the same game, end on a **capability payoff** or a **reversal** (host
out-absurds the caller — Chris Head's reversal ending, Part A4). Native-format camouflage
and outrage/curiosity hooks (Part A6) apply directly.

## Part C — AI-generated characters (both formats)

This is where the format's usual charm ("same real person plays everyone" / "a real host
you bond with") needs deliberate replacement. The evidence is **mixed, not damning** — and
the strongest anti-AI claims were *killed* in verification.

### C1. What survived verification
- **Virtual (AI) influencers CAN enhance brand engagement**, but perceptions are
  constrained by **authenticity, transparency, and the uncanny-valley effect.**
  [ScienceDirect systematic review, 51 studies]
- **Four determinants of effectiveness:** consistent digital authenticity, balanced
  disclosure, appropriate human-likeness, cultural tailoring. (2-1 vote.) [ScienceDirect]
- **Mitigate uncanny-valley + skepticism with a coherent, transparent digital identity** —
  a stable, openly-virtual persona rather than passing as human. [ScienceDirect]
- **Deliberate imperfection heuristic:** flawless, over-optimized AI aesthetics can read as
  artificial. [extracted; treat as an A/B heuristic, not law] — corroborated by the
  human-creator finding that less-polished branded content out-performed polished (A2).
- **Digital literacy moderates reactions:** high-literacy audiences react less negatively
  to AI content. [extracted]

### C2. Disclosure — genuinely contested
- **Journal of Interactive Advertising (2025), N=304, between-subjects** [Koning &
  Voorveld, U. Amsterdam]: an AI-generated-content **disclosure decreased trust** in the
  ad and the organization (via raised AI/persuasion knowledge) — **but also produced a
  countervailing positive path** where persuasion knowledge **increased** trust. Net
  effect is not simply negative.
- **U. Manchester (2025)** conference paper: tests AI-disclosure × beliefs-about-AI-
  capability on trust/authenticity/purchase-intention — implies the effect **depends on
  what viewers believe AI can do.** (Abstract-level only; page was 403.)
- ⚠️ **KILLED claims:** "AI influencers significantly reduce authenticity/brand trust vs
  humans" and "explicit AI disclosure *exacerbates* the trust penalty." Both were refuted
  in adversarial verification (single-source, over-generalized) — **do not treat as
  settled.**
- **Practical stance for the skill:** disclosure effects are real but mixed and context-/
  jurisdiction-dependent. Check local ad rules; where you disclose, do it as part of a
  confident, stylized, openly-virtual brand, not an apologetic asterisk. Lean on
  stylization + a stable likeable persona over fake-realism.

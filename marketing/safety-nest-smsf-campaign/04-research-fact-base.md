## A. The core mechanic — validated ✅ (your assumption, sharpened)

**A1. Rolling from an APRA fund to an SMSF loses important protections — regulator-stated.** (HIGH, 3-0)
ASIC 25-265MR (6 Nov 2025): *"People who move their super from an APRA-regulated fund
to an SMSF lose important protections, including the benefits of prudential regulation
and the ability to make a complaint about the fund or its trustees to AFCA."*
ASIC's REP 824 package separately flags **loss of insurance cover held in the previous
fund** — and failure to properly consider replacement cover (exclusions, premium
loadings) — as a risk of SMSF establishment that advisers frequently fail to address.
- https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2025-releases/25-265mr-asic-review-raises-fresh-concerns-over-risks-to-retirement-savings-from-poor-smsf-advice/
- https://download.asic.gov.au/media/g2jloagp/rep824-published-6-november-2025.pdf
- ⚠️ Copy caution: ASIC frames insurance loss as a *recognised risk*, not a prevalence
  statistic. Say "ASIC flags loss of existing cover as a key risk of SMSF establishment,"
  never "insurance is commonly lost." The lose-protections quote itself does not mention
  insurance — don't imply it does.

**A2. The "keep a small balance in the old fund" workaround fails by default — this is the campaign's sharpest hook.** (HIGH, 3-0)
Under Protecting Your Super (SIS Act **s68AAA**, since 1 July 2019), death/TPD cover in
the old fund is **automatically cancelled after 16 continuous months without
contributions or rollovers**, even if a balance remains, unless the member lodges a
**written opt-in election**. Funds must send warning notices at 9, 12 and 15 months
(often to stale email addresses). Preserving cover requires either a contribution/
rollover (resets the 16-month clock) **or** the one-off written opt-in — a parked
balance alone does nothing. Note: balance thresholds (<$6,000) belong to the separate
Putting Members' Interests First rules, not s68AAA.
- https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/sia1993473/s68aaa.html
- https://www.apra.gov.au/protecting-your-super-package-frequently-asked-questions
- https://moneysmart.gov.au/how-super-works/self-managed-super-fund-smsf
- https://www.heffron.com.au/knowledge-centre/insurance-in-smsfs

**A3. The partial-rollover strategy is standard practice — so the failure mode is widespread.** (HIGH, 3-0)
Keeping a second account in the old fund purely to retain group cover is a common,
deliberate SMSF-trustee strategy — group premiums in large funds are typically cheaper
than individual policies, especially with health conditions that make re-underwriting
hard. That's exactly the population s68AAA silently cancels.

**A4. Regulatory tailwind: poor SMSF-establishment advice is under active ASIC scrutiny.** (HIGH, 3-0)
REP 824 (Nov 2025): of 100 risk-targeted SMSF-establishment advice files, **62 failed
to demonstrate best-interests-duty compliance; 27 raised significant client-detriment
concerns**. Commissioner Alan Kirkland: ASIC is *"considering a range of regulatory
responses, including enforcement action."* Precedent: April 2025 $11m Federal Court
penalty against Equiti Financial Services for cookie-cutter SMSF advice.
- ⚠️ **Refuted framing (0-3):** the sample was risk-targeted, NOT representative. Never
  present 62% as the failure rate of SMSF advice generally.

**A5. Illustrative story (vendor anecdote — use as illustration only, never as documented case).** (MEDIUM, 3-0)
Grow SMSF (Apr 2026) tells of "Brandon", 44, self-employed NSW architect, father of
three: $358,000 death/TPD cover in his old industry fund at ~$350/yr; left a balance
behind, received the three statutory warnings, deposited $200 — still lost cover and
died in 2023 before the family discovered the cancellation. The article's fixes:
(a) annual $200–$500 contribution before the 16-month mark + the fund's "keep your
cover" written election, or (b) transfer cover into the SMSF via a specialist broker.
- https://growsmsf.com.au/smsf-trustees-dont-lose-insurance-in-your-old-super-fund/
- ⚠️ Single vendor blog; internal timing tension (a $200 deposit should reset the
  clock under s68AAA). If used in creative, label clearly as an illustrative scenario
  (RG 234) or replace with a substantiated first-party case study.

## B. Advertising compliance — validated ✅ (see 05 for operating rules)

**B1.** Safety Nest's paid digital is squarely within **RG 234** scope (promoters and
publishers of financial-product advertising; life insurance is a financial product,
Corporations Act s764A). Test = **overall impression on first viewing** by an ordinary,
reasonable member of the audience. (HIGH, 3-0)

**B2.** Character-limited formats: banner/search ads should state offer restrictions
**within the ad itself** — a link to fine print is unlikely to cure a misleading
impression. Disclaimers can't rescue a misleading headline. Premium claims must give a
realistic impression of total cost. (HIGH, 3-0)

**B3.** **RG 234 was reissued 9 June 2026** (first major rework since 2012): consolidates
RG 53 (withdrawn), adds greenwashing guidance, distinguishes required warnings from
disclaimers, shifts emphasis to consumer understanding/prominence/balance. No transition
period — applies now. Use only the 9 June 2026 edition for compliance checks.
- https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf

## D. What this means for the campaign (strategist's note)

1. **The premise survives scrutiny — and improves.** The naive version ("you lose your
   insurance when you leave") is only half the story. The verified version is better
   copy: *even the people who thought they were being clever* (partial rollover) lose
   cover 16 months later by statutory default. Problem-unaware × false-security = ideal
   direct-response conditions.
2. **Two legally-grounded urgency mechanisms exist** (Kennedy's "reason to respond now"
   that RG 234 permits because they're TRUE): the running 16-month s68AAA clock on every
   recent rollover, and health/age-driven insurability.
3. **The regulator is the campaign's credibility engine.** ASIC/REP 824 language can be
   quoted (accurately, with the cautions above) instead of scare statistics.
4. **Compliance is a design constraint from day one**, not a legal review at the end:
   qualifications live in the ad unit itself, headlines must survive the first-viewing
   test, and the 9 June 2026 RG 234 is the reference text.

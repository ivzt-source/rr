# Safety Nest — Video Creative Concepts

> Creative options for the Meta "Reveal" and YouTube "Documentary" layers
> (see `02-campaign-architecture.md`). Option C is the 80s-nostalgia cartoon
> concept (producer: Merav). Every script ships only after the
> `05-compliance-guardrails.md` pre-flight checklist.

## Option A — "The Reveal" (talking-head UGC)
Straight-to-camera trustee story: rolled over, found out at audit time the cover was
gone. Cheap, fast, the control against which other creative is tested.

## Option B — "The 16-Month Clock" (kinetic type / motion graphics)
A literal countdown aesthetic on the s68AAA mechanics. Rational, compliance-flavored,
strong for retargeting where the viewer already knows the premise.

## Option C — "Saturday Morning" (80s cartoon with voice-over) ⭐ nostalgia play

### Why it fits the verified avatar
- Largest new-trustee cohort is **35–44, median 46** (born ~1976–1991): their
  childhood TV window is **~1983–1995 Saturday-morning cel animation** — He-Man,
  Transformers, Voltron, ThunderCats, Astro Boy reruns, Danger Mouse, Inspector
  Gadget, TMNT, Captain Planet. Aim the aesthetic at that band, not strictly "the 80s."
- **68% of SMSFs are couples' funds** and the primary target is the father/breadwinner.
  The nostalgia device does double duty Kennedy would approve of: it *is* the pattern
  interrupt (nobody else in this category runs cartoon creative — the whole SERP is
  stock-photo families), and it *is* the message — you watched heroes protect people;
  now you're the one your kids watch.
- Emotional mechanism: put the man in his **children's** shoes by putting him back in
  his own. The product is never the hero; *he* is. Safety Nest is the shield.

### The concept in one line
> In the cartoons you grew up on, the hero never went into battle without his shield.
> You just did — your SMSF rollover dropped it — and the little people watching from
> the couch are counting on you.

### Master VO script — 60s hero cut ("The Shield")

*(Visual style: original cel-animation pastiche, 4:3 frame with VHS tracking
artifacts, synth score. VO: warm, gravelly "movie-trailer dad" voice.)*

| Time | Visual | VO |
|---|---|---|
| 0–8s | Cold open INSIDE the cartoon: a caped hero on a neon cliff, synth sting, title card "DEFENDER OF THE NEST — as seen every Saturday, 7am" | "Saturday mornings. A bowl of cereal. And a hero who never — ever — lost." |
| 8–18s | Hero battles a storm-cloud villain, protecting a glowing nest of small creatures; freeze-frame mid-leap | "He had a fortress. He had a plan. And he had a shield. You loved that guy… because he protected the little guys." |
| 18–30s | Match cut: the hero's silhouette becomes a real-world dad (rotoscope style) at a kitchen table with SMSF paperwork; kids watching cartoons on the couch behind him | "These days, the hero is you. You even built your own fortress — a self-managed super fund. Smart move." |
| 30–42s | The cartoon shield pixelates and vanishes off the dad's arm; a VHS "tracking" glitch; the couch kids look over | "But here's what nobody told you: when you rolled out of your old fund, your life insurance didn't come with you. The shield stayed behind. And after 16 months, it's usually cancelled — even if you left money there." |
| 42–52s | Style shifts back to full cartoon: dad picks up a new, brighter shield stamped with the Safety Nest mark; nest glows | "Safety Nest is life cover built for SMSF trustees. Ten minutes to check your gap. Because the little guys on the couch think you're invincible." |
| 52–60s | End card, 80s toy-ad framing: "SAFETY NEST — Free SMSF Insurance Gap Check" + general advice warning, AFSL line, prominent and static | "Safety Nest. Protect what you built. Protect who you built it for. Free gap check — link below." |

### Cutdowns
- **30s (mid-funnel):** open at the match cut (18s), keep glitch reveal + end card.
- **15s (retargeting):** shield-vanish glitch + "Your rollover dropped your shield.
  16-month clock's running. Free gap check." + end card.
- **6s bumper (YouTube):** freeze-frame hero + VO: "Still the hero? Check your shield.
  Safety Nest."
- **9:16 verticals:** reframe key scenes; captions burned in (85% of Meta views are
  muted — the VHS aesthetic makes stylized subtitles feel native, like a taped-off-TV
  recording).

### Alternate angles to test within the same world
1. **"The Episode You Missed"** — fake "lost episode" framing: "In the episode nobody
   saw, the hero left his shield at the old fortress…" Leans harder into intrigue.
2. **"From the Couch"** — shot entirely from the kids' POV watching the screen; the
   cartoon hero starts resembling their dad. Strongest emotional variant; test against
   the hero cut.
3. **B2B spin-off (LinkedIn, 30s)** — same animation world: the hero's *armorer*
   (the accountant) hands over the fortress keys but isn't allowed to hand him a
   shield. "You set up the fortress. The shield isn't your department — but the
   referral can be." Reuses assets, speaks the licensing truth.

### Production notes for Merav
- **Style, not IP:** original characters and world only — an "in the style of
  mid-80s/early-90s syndicated cartoon" pastiche (limited animation, cel shading,
  painted backgrounds, 12fps action, chromatic bloom, tape grain). Do **not** imitate
  any specific show's characters, logos, title cards or theme melody (He-Man,
  Transformers, etc. are aggressively protected marks). A generic "Saturday morning"
  feel is safe; a recognizable Eternia is not.
- **Sound:** commission or license a synthwave cue (DX7 brass, gated reverb drums);
  no soundalikes of specific theme songs. VO casting: male, 45–60, warm authority.
- **Routes:** (a) boutique animation studio doing retro cel pastiche; (b) AI-assisted
  animation (style-consistent gen video + cleanup) for the test cut, upgrading to
  studio animation if the concept wins; (c) hybrid — animate only 4–5 hero shots,
  bridge with pans over painted stills (authentically 80s and cheap).
- **Test cheap first (Kennedy rule):** cut a 30s animatic (stills + VO + music) and
  run it as a Meta thumb-stop test against Options A/B before funding full animation.
  Decision metric: 3-second hold rate and CPL, not compliments.

### Compliance notes (RG 234 — see 05)
- The metaphor must not overpromise: the shield = *cover you can apply for*, subject
  to underwriting — the end card and landing page must carry the qualifications; the
  cartoon itself should avoid absolute claims ("always protected", "invincible" said
  *by* the product). "The little guys think you're invincible" is aimed at the dad,
  not the product — keep it that way.
- The 16-month line in the script states the verified s68AAA mechanic with "usually" —
  keep the qualifier; it's cancellation *unless* contributions/opt-in.
- General advice warning + AFSL details on the end card, static and legible, not
  styled into illegibility by the VHS filter.
- Nostalgia is an emotional appeal, not a misleading one — fine under RG 234 — but the
  *overall impression on first viewing* must still be accurate: an SMSF rollover
  usually leaves prior insurance behind; a check is free; cover is subject to
  eligibility.

### Where it slots in the funnel
- **Primary:** Meta Hook C slot (advertorial/story) and YouTube pre-roll — the
  problem-unaware audience where a pattern interrupt earns its CPM.
- **Retargeting:** 15s glitch cut.
- **B2B:** the armorer spin-off inside the LinkedIn Referral Engine.
- KPI treatment identical to other creative: it's an option in the test matrix, not
  the campaign. If "Saturday Morning" beats the UGC control on CPL at equal spend,
  scale it; if not, the animatic cost was the tuition.

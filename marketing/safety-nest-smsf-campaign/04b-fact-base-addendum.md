### New-entrant demographics (the avatar, verified)
- **Median age at establishment: 46** (funds established 2023–24, ATO annual stats;
  down from 51 for 2006–2014 vintages). Class platform data: average new-member age 48
  vs 61.6 across all members *(single source: Class)*.
- **Largest new-member cohort: 35–44 (≈39%)**; >50% of new members are under 50
  (Dec 2025 & Mar 2026 quarters). ⚠️ secondary write-ups of ATO tables.
- **Most common taxable income of new entrants: $100k–$150k (≈26%)**, next band
  $150k–$200k (≈14%). ⚠️ same caveat.
- Fund composition: **68% two-member funds** (couples), 25% single-member (30 Jun 2025,
  via SuperGuide summary of ATO annual stats — single-sourced as retrieved).
- Average assets per fund ~$1.63M / per member ~$881k (30 Jun 2024, ATO annual);
  **average balance AT establishment fell 29% to ~$363k in FY25** (Class Benchmark,
  single source) — new trustees are younger and start smaller, i.e. LESS able to
  self-insure, strengthening the product case.

### Insurance coverage gap (the whitespace)
- Cooper Review (2010): **fewer than 13% of SMSFs held insurance for members** — the
  origin of the oft-cited 13% (sometimes misattributed to ASIC REP 413).
- Best recent estimate: **~15%** of SMSFs hold life cover for members (industry
  consultant, May 2024, single source; figure skewed by aged membership and cover held
  outside the fund).
- Copy rule: cite as "industry estimates suggest only around 13–15% of SMSFs hold life
  insurance for their members" with vintage; never as a current official statistic.

## B. SIS Reg 4.09 — the compliance hook (verified)

- Reg 4.09(2) SIS Regulations: trustees **"must formulate, review regularly and give
  effect to"** an investment strategy having regard to the whole circumstances of the
  fund including **(e) "whether the trustees of the fund should hold a contract of
  insurance that provides insurance cover for one or more members of the fund."**
  (AustLII text; DBA Lawyers; SMSF Association; Cleardocs — multi-sourced.)
- The insurance limb applies to SMSFs and dates from **7 Aug 2012** ⚠️ (date is
  secondary-sourced, widely repeated).
- **Every SMSF is audited annually** by an ASIC-registered auditor whose compliance
  engagement includes reg 4.09; failures can trigger an Auditor Contravention Report
  to the ATO. The requirement is to *consider and document*, not to *hold*, insurance.
- **ATO enforcement precedent:** Aug–Sep 2019 letters to ~17,700 trustees about
  investment-strategy compliance (auditors copied), flagging a then-$4,200
  administrative penalty.
- **Penalty today:** breach of the operating standard = 20 penalty units × $330
  (from 7 Nov 2024) = **$6,600 per individual trustee**, not payable from fund assets.
- Campaign use: this is the compliance urgency for both avatars — trustees must
  answer the insurance question in writing every year, and auditors ask it.

## C. Accountants & licensing — the referral wedge (verified)

- **Accountants' exemption (Corps Reg 7.1.29A) repealed 1 July 2016.** Since then,
  recommending SMSF establishment/wind-up is financial product advice requiring AFSL
  coverage. (ASIC INFO 216; 16-425MR; CPA Australia; Cleardocs — multi-sourced.)
- **Unlicensed accountants cannot recommend specific insurance products.** Even
  limited AFS licensees max out at class-of-product advice (life insurance class
  requires extra training); specific-product recommendations need full AFSL coverage.
- **What they CAN do:** traditional compliance/admin work; give factual information;
  tax-consequences advice (reg 7.1.29(4), incidental, no product recommendation);
  execute documentation on client instruction ⚠️ (contested edge — INFO 216 is the
  safe line); and **make "mere referrals"**.
- **Referral mechanics (Corps Reg 7.6.01(1)(e)/(ea)):** the referrer may only say the
  licensee can provide certain services and how to contact them; **any benefit to the
  referrer must be disclosed at the time of referral**; passing client details needs
  client consent. Referral fees are permitted with disclosure. (Adviser-side: Code of
  Ethics Standard 3 constrains what advisers may pay ⚠️ single-sourced.)
- **The limited-licence regime failed**, leaving a service vacuum: 787 limited AFS
  licences at 30 Jun 2017; advisers under the limited model fell from ~2,787 (2018)
  to **~339 (Aug 2025, −88%)** ⚠️ (SMSF Adviser, single source). Most accountants who
  set up SMSFs today are unlicensed and structurally UNABLE to solve their clients'
  insurance gap themselves — they need a compliant referral path. **This validates the
  B2B campaign thesis.**
- **Anti-hawking (Corps Act s992A, from 5 Oct 2021; ASIC RG 38):** no unsolicited
  real-time offers of insurance; consumer-initiated contact and properly consented
  referrals are outside the prohibition. Design the referral kit so the CLIENT
  initiates or gives positive, clear consent before any Safety Nest contact.

"you lost your cover when you rolled over" message.** The fact is ubiquitous as
*educational* content (SuperGuide, Heffron, Grow SMSF, Real Insurance FAQs) but nobody
owns it as a campaign position. Closest: Grow SMSF's blog article (an admin firm, not
an insurer) and one paragraph in a NobleOak insights piece. The Reg 4.09 compliance

### D3. Platform rules (AU, current)

- **Google Ads:** since 30 Aug 2022, ALL financial-services advertisers targeting
  Australia need **Financial Services Verification** — third-party check by G2 Risk
  Solutions proving AFSL authorisation or exemption (~5 days), then Google identity
  verification. Insurance is in scope and allowed once verified. **Start this before
  any media plan — it gates launch.**
- **Meta:** from ~4 Feb 2025, AU-targeted financial-products ads require advertiser
  verification (AFSL number or declared exemption) via the beneficiary-and-payer
  Compliance Check; ads carry a clickable "Paid for By" disclaimer with the licence
  number, visible in Ad Library. 18+ targeting mandatory. (AU is NOT under the US
  special-ad-category targeting lock, per available docs ⚠️.)
- **LinkedIn:** job title / job function ("Accounting") / member-skills (e.g.
  "Self-Managed Superannuation Funds", verify audience size in Campaign Manager) /
  groups / industry targeting; 300-member minimum, practical floor ~50k for sponsored
  content. No AU AFSL-verification requirement found (UK FCA rule exists; AU absence
  is inference ⚠️).

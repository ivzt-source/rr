# Safety Nest — Advertising Compliance Guardrails (ASIC RG 234, 9 June 2026 edition)

> Every ad, landing page, email and lead magnet ships only after passing this checklist.
> RG 234 is ASIC's good-practice guidance; the binding prohibitions on misleading or
> deceptive conduct sit in the Corporations Act and ASIC Act and apply regardless.
> Reference text: RG 234 as reissued **9 June 2026** (RG 53 withdrawn and consolidated
> into it; no transition period). Pre-2026 summaries and paragraph numbers are stale.

## The governing test

**Overall impression on FIRST viewing** by an ordinary, reasonable member of the ad's
audience. Consumers are not expected to study, re-read, or click through. If the
headline alone would mislead that person, the ad fails — no disclaimer rescues it.

## Hard rules for our formats

### Search / banner / character-limited ads
- Material restrictions and qualifications must appear **in the ad unit itself**.
  "See site for details" does not cure a misleading impression.
- If the format physically can't carry balanced information, RG 234 (2026) says
  reconsider whether the medium is appropriate for that message — simplify the claim
  until it's self-sufficient, or don't run it in that format.

### Headlines and hooks
- Insurance ads must not create a false impression that cover is available without
  qualifications (underwriting, exclusions, waiting periods, age limits).
- Warnings/disclaimers must be **consistent with** the headline (a disclaimer that
  contradicts the hook is evidence of the problem, not a fix) and **prominent on
  first viewing** — same-screen, legible, not grey-on-grey footer text.

### Price and premium claims
- Quoted premiums must give a **realistic impression of total cost** over time.
  (ASIC challenged "cut your car insurance into 12 easy monthly payments" where the
  12 payments exceeded the annual premium.) If monthly pricing is shown, the effective
  annual cost basis must be clear; stepped-premium products must not read as level.

### Statistics and regulator references
- ASIC REP 824's 62/100 figure came from a **risk-targeted** sample: never present it
  as the failure rate of SMSF advice generally (that framing was refuted 0-3 in
  verification).
- Frame insurance loss as a **risk ASIC flags**, not a prevalence claim: ✅ "ASIC flags
  loss of existing cover as a key risk of SMSF establishment" / ❌ "most SMSF trustees
  lose their insurance."
- Don't attribute the insurance-risk language to the ASIC media release itself; cite
  the REP 824 guidance package.

### Case studies and testimonials
- "Brandon"-style stories: only run substantiated first-party cases, or label clearly
  as illustrative scenarios. No invented specificity (names, sums, dates) presented
  as fact.

### Urgency (Kennedy's "reason to respond now" — only TRUE deadlines)
- ✅ The statutory 16-month s68AAA inactivity clock on a recent rollover.
- ✅ Age-banded premium changes and health-dependent insurability.
- ✅ Genuine offer end-dates we actually enforce.
- ❌ Fake countdowns, fake scarcity, implied regulatory deadlines that don't exist.

### Product boundary
- Safety Nest marketing is **general advice / factual information** unless an AFSL
  personal-advice flow exists: include the general-advice warning ("doesn't take into
  account your objectives, financial situation or needs…") prominently, not buried.
- Anti-hawking: no unsolicited outbound contact offering the product; inbound funnel
  responses and consented follow-up only. Consent language in lead forms must cover
  the nurture sequence we actually send.
- B2B accountant materials must not induce unlicensed accountants to give product
  advice — the referral kit's whole premise is keeping them on the right side of the
  AFSL line.

## Pre-flight checklist (every asset)

1. First-viewing test: would the headline alone mislead? (Have someone outside the
   team read it for 5 seconds and say what they think is being offered.)
2. Are all material qualifications inside the ad unit?
3. Is every number sourced to the fact base (`04-research-fact-base.md` / addendum)
   with vintage noted, and framed within its verified caveats?
4. Is any urgency claim literally true and provable?
5. Is the general-advice warning present and prominent where required?
6. Premium/cost claims: realistic total-cost impression?
7. Case studies substantiated or labelled illustrative?
8. Sign-off recorded (who approved, date, asset version) — ASIC expects promoters to
   be able to substantiate claims at the time they're made.

## B. The insurance hole in the stock

- **Cooper Review (2010):** <13% of SMSFs held member insurance.
- **Plan for Life (Dec 2014):** **84% of SMSF members aged 18–64 held NO life
  insurance of any kind** — 630,000+ uncovered members. ⚠️ **11 years old — cite as
  "long-standing gap" with its date, never as current data.**
- **ASIC REP 576 (Jun 2018, member survey):** 19% of SMSF members didn't consider
  insurance when setting up; 30% had no arrangements if something happened to them.
  **ASIC REP 575 (Jun 2018, advice-file review):** 91% of 250 reviewed SMSF advice
  files breached best-interests obligations. (Keep the report numbers straight in
  any citation.)
- **Recent estimate:** ~15% of funds hold member cover (SMS Magazine, May 2024 ⚠️).
  Note the fund-level vs member-level distinction: the ~13%/15% figures are *funds*,
  the 84% is *members 18–64* — don't mix them in one claim.
- **"Ghost cover" is a sized behaviour:** Investment Trends (Mar 2017 ⚠️) — **one-third
  of trustees who keep money in an APRA fund do so for the cheaper insurance** — the
  exact population whose cover PYS has been cancelling since July 2019.
- **Underinsurance framing stats (Rice Warner):** 2020 — aggregate cover meets only
  92% of basic death-cover needs and **29% of TPD needs**; a 30-yr-old couple with
  kids needs 9–12× the higher earner's income in basic death cover. Usable as
  category framing with dates.
- **Product age cut-offs reinforce the targeting window:** typical entry-age caps
  ~55 (IP) and ~60 (death/TPD) — another reason the honest audience ends before 60.
- **Not found anywhere (do not claim):** average age cover is dropped; longitudinal
  lapse-after-establishment data; % of strategies that address insurance.

## C. "Set and forget" — the evidence

> **Offer-design rule (from the client):** a general "investment strategy review" is a
> weak, commoditised offer — trustees already have accountants/advisers for that. The
> facts below are **proof and urgency for the INSURANCE piece only**: the offer is
> always the insurance gap check / the missing insurance clause / the cover transfer —
> never a strategy audit.

- Reg 4.09 requires the strategy be **"regularly reviewed"**; ATO guidance: review
  **at least annually**, minute the review even if nothing changes, and re-review on
  significant events (member joins/leaves/dies, pension starts, market correction,
  relationship breakdown).
- Confirmed via the amending instrument's Explanatory Statement (SLI No 183 of 2012):
  the insurance limb commenced **7 Aug 2012**, purpose "to require trustees of SMSFs
  to consider insurance for their members" — now multi-sourced (upgrades the ⚠️ on
  this date in 04b).
- ATO published dedicated guidance in **Feb 2020** (after the letter campaign) —
  0–100% ranges invalid; strategy must not "just repeat the words in the legislation".
- Auditor commentary: strategies **making no mention of insurance at all are a
  standard audit finding** (no published percentage exists — don't quantify).
- **ATO rejects template strategies**: "not a valid approach to merely specify
  investment ranges of 0 to 100%" (Asst Commissioner Dana Fleming) — which is exactly
  what many setup-day documents say.
- 2019: ATO wrote to **~17,700 funds** with >90% in one asset class (**~98% were
  LRBA-property funds**) and noted **~180,000 funds (~1 in 3) held 90%+ in a single
  asset class**. Auditors were copied. Net administrative penalties raised rose from
  $1.7M (2017–18) to $3.1M (2018–19) ⚠️. (Follow-up compliance action was light ⚠️ —
  use the letters as the precedent, not an active-enforcement claim.)
- Auditor commentary: strategies "not updated to reflect a change in member
  circumstances (retirement, marriage breakdown, death)" are among the most common
  audit issues. ACR stats FY22–23: 15,200 funds reported, 41,200 contraventions —
  but reg 4.09's specific share isn't published (gap; don't quantify in copy).

## D. "Take it over" — transfer/replacement mechanics (shapes the offer AND the copy)

**The honest differentiator: "Don't cancel — transfer."**

- **Takeover terms exist as a product**: e.g. the AIA-backed SMSF master-plan offers an
  Individual Transfer Option matching existing cover **with original underwriting
  carried over — no new medicals** (incl. accepted pre-existing conditions and
  already-served IP waiting periods) up to $2M death/TPD, $20k/mo IP ⚠️ (one
  provider's caps — verify per insurer).
- **Personally owned → SMSF:** no direct transfer; insurers do cancel-and-reissue to
  the trustee **carrying the original underwriting position** where offered ⚠️
  (insurer-dependent).
- **Group cover (APRA fund) → SMSF: confirmed impossible** on the same terms — new
  cover must be arranged, ideally before rollover.
- **Replacement risks (why churn framing is banned from our copy):** a fresh policy
  at 50+ = full re-underwriting (declines/loadings/exclusions), 13-month suicide
  exclusion restarts (waived only under strict conditions), IP waiting-period and
  pre-existing-condition positions reset. ASIC's REP 413/587 history and the Life
  Insurance Code of Practice make anti-churn posture both ethical and strategic.
- **What can't move into super at all (since 1 Jul 2014, reg 4.07D):** new trauma and
  own-occupation TPD — pre-2014 policies are grandfathered. Screen for this in the
  review tool.
- **Premium economics — the honest targeting cut-off:** a 55-year-old pays ~5× a
  25-year-old; cheapest $500k life cover ≈ $39–50/mo at 50 vs ~$164–240/mo at 60 ⚠️;
  stepped premiums roughly double per decade and steepen past 50. **45–55 is the
  window where fixing cover still pays; new stepped cover at 60+ is rarely economic**
  (inference — do not state as fact in copy). Deductibility to the fund: death and
  any-occupation TPD 100%, IP deductible.
- **"Considered and decided against" is compliant** — auditors accept a reasoned,
  member-named "no". What fails audit is silence. (This makes the rewrite offer
  honest: we're selling the *documented answer*, and cover only where it genuinely fits.)

## E. Copy-accuracy traps specific to this avatar

1. **PYS never applied to SMSFs themselves.** The s68AAA 16-month cancellation applies
   to *APRA-fund* accounts. The legitimate angle for established trustees: the old
   APRA account many kept alive for cover has likely had that cover cancelled since
   July 2019. ✅ "If you kept an old fund account for the insurance, check it — the
   law changed in 2019." ❌ "PYS cancelled your SMSF's insurance."
2. The 84% stat is Dec-2014 vintage; the 13% stat is 2010. Date them or drop them.
3. No public data on age-based lapse or cover-drop timing — never imply it.
4. Wind-up counts for the latest 1–2 FYs are understated until returns lodge.

## G. Verdict on the "quickest to cash" hypothesis

**Supported, with a targeting condition.** The stock (~536k funds >5yrs old) dwarfs
the annual flow (~40k), ~79% are unadvised, the structure already exists (no setup
lag), an annual audit forcing-event is built into every fund's calendar, and takeover
products mean qualified prospects can move without new underwriting. The condition:
the honest pool is the **wholly-accumulation, under-60 cohort (~370k funds, avg member
age 52)** — pension-phase 60+ members are the majority of the stock's *members* and
mostly poor insurance prospects. Target the fund's *phase and age band*, not the
segment as a whole, or CPLs will look good while underwriting and conversion collapse.
